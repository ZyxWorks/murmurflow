MurmurFlow

Hold a key.
Talk. Let go.

The text appears at your cursor: Slack, a terminal, your notes. Nothing you say leaves your machine.

Install

macOS, paste into Terminal

/bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hannesreinsch/murmurflow/main/install.sh)"

Windows, paste into PowerShell

irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hannesreinsch/murmurflow/main/install.ps1 | iex

Either one works on a machine with nothing on it: no package manager, no Python, no developer tools, and on Windows no Administrator either. It installs what is missing, downloads the speech model once, and turns dictation on for every login.

Then hold Control+Option, say something, let go. That is the whole product.

What it is

Localwhisper.cpp on your own machine. No account, no server, no telemetry.
FreeMIT licensed. No subscription, and nothing to cancel.
No dependenciesThe Python standard library, ffmpeg, and the OS. Nothing else.
EverywhereAny app that takes a paste, which is all of them.

Speed, measured

stepseconds
first transcription after boot, cold13.3
first transcription, warm server2.3
microphone open, first ever9.9
microphone open, thereafter0.3

M4 Pro, macOS 26, large-v3-turbo, an 11 second clip. The warm server earns its place on the first sentence after a boot, which is the one a person forms their opinion on, so the daemon pays that cost at login while nobody is waiting. Measure your own before believing any of these, including ours.

What it does not do

  • No compiled helper. Key state is polled through stdlib ctypes. No Xcode, no signing, no notarization.
  • No streaming. It would cost a permanently resident microphone to save a fraction of a batch pass.
  • No LLM on the hot path, unless you ask for one.
  • No Linux. Wayland exposes no global hotkey API at all, so the answer is honest rather than half working.

Privacy, concretely

Audio is written to ~/.murmurflow/audio/ and deleted the instant it is transcribed, before the optional polish call and before the paste, so a crash downstream cannot leave your voice on disk. The recorder bounds its own life at ten minutes, so a daemon killed mid-clip cannot leave the microphone hot.

The transcript is never inspected, filtered, or written down. It goes to your clipboard, then your cursor, and your previous clipboard contents are put back. Nothing here makes a network request except the one-off model download.
